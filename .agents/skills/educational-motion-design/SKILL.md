# Educational Motion Design Skill

## Purpose

Create clear, visually rich educational explainers using a curated visual vocabulary of 15 styles. The reference is a 2:51, 1280×720, 30 fps Chinese-language design showcase. It alternates a consistent editorial chapter layout with a different visual treatment for each topic.

The goal is **not** to force every scene into one template. Preserve a coherent overall identity while selecting the visual style that best explains each idea.

## Core creative principles

1. **Teach first, decorate second.** Every visual must explain, compare, sequence, locate, or reinforce an idea.
2. **One primary idea per shot.** Avoid stacking several unrelated facts in one frame.
3. **Make abstract ideas concrete.** Use objects, scale comparisons, maps, diagrams, metaphors, timelines, and visual transformations.
4. **Use contrast deliberately.** Alternate dark cinematic scenes, warm paper-like scenes, and clean light instructional scenes to maintain rhythm.
5. **Keep text concise.** Use short headings, labels, and callouts. Narration carries detail that would otherwise overcrowd the frame.
6. **Build motion into the explanation.** Animate the transformation, reveal, comparison, route, assembly, or sequence—not random decorative movement.
7. **Maintain a visual hierarchy.** One dominant visual, one clear title, supporting labels, and restrained explanatory copy.
8. **Use style as meaning.** Choose shadow theatre for folklore, isometric diagrams for processes, whiteboard drawing for reasoning, pixel art for game/history references, and so on.
9. **Respect source facts.** Do not invent statistics, dates, labels, or scientific relationships. Mark uncertain or user-supplied claims for verification.
10. **Design for 16:9 first.** The reference is 1280×720. Keep essential text and objects inside safe margins so the work can be adapted to other formats.

## Reference visual identity

### Overall composition

The showcase repeatedly uses an editorial chapter frame:
- A small chapter number, often orange, near the upper-left.
- A large Chinese chapter heading beside or below it.
- A short block of explanatory copy beneath the heading.
- A large demonstration window occupying the right or central-right portion.
- A fine top-edge metadata/caption line in some scenes.
- Backgrounds are often softly blurred, vignetted, or tinted to harmonize with the demonstration window.
- The demonstration window may look like a framed screen, paper sheet, dark data panel, illustration board, or embedded video.

This frame is a navigation system, not a mandatory layout for every shot. Full-screen scenes and close-up demonstrations are used when they improve comprehension.

### Palette

The exact source color values are not recoverable from the video alone. Use these approximate visual roles, then tune by eye:
- **Near-black / charcoal:** cinematic backgrounds and title cards.
- **Deep navy:** data visualization, space/scale comparisons, timelines, technical graphics.
- **Warm parchment / ivory:** maps, hand-drawn diagrams, historical or archival content.
- **Muted grey / taupe:** neutral backdrops and soft vignette transitions.
- **Burnt orange / vermilion:** chapter numbers, emphasis, key words, highlights.
- **Cream / off-white:** high-contrast typography and diagram surfaces.
- **Muted greens and sky blues:** nature, geography, process diagrams, science illustrations.
- **Gold / amber:** dates, important figures, focal points, premium accents.

Keep accent colors selective. Orange is a recurring signal color; it should not become a full-frame default.

### Typography

The exact typeface cannot be identified reliably from the compressed video. Reproduce the *typographic character*, not an unsupported claim about the original font:
- **Main headings:** bold, condensed or compact sans-serif; strong weight; clear strokes; high legibility.
- **Chapter numerals / key figures:** large, elegant serif or high-contrast display numeral treatment can be used where the scene calls for a premium editorial feel.
- **Body copy:** clean sans-serif, regular/medium weight, compact line spacing.
- **Labels and annotations:** small sans-serif; high contrast; restrained tracking.
- **Emphasis:** use orange/red blocks, underlines, colored keywords, or scale contrast rather than multiple font families.
- **CJK text:** choose a well-hinted Chinese sans-serif with consistent simplified/traditional glyph coverage if producing Chinese. For English, use a neutral modern grotesk/sans-serif pairing.
- Use no more than two principal font families per scene. Establish a consistent heading/body system across the whole project.
- Do not imitate illegible tiny text. At 1280×720, body copy should remain comfortably readable; if the frame is viewed on mobile, simplify further.

### Texture, lighting, and depth

- Soft vignette and blurred color fields behind framed content.
- Warm paper grain for historical, map, and hand-drawn scenes.
- Subtle shadows around inset frames and cards.
- Cinematic spotlighting for object reveals and dark-background diagrams.
- Flat vector art for process and educational diagrams.
- Restrained 3D lighting and soft contact shadows for exploded objects.
- Avoid excessive bloom, glossy gradients, and generic template effects.

### Motion language

Use purposeful, controlled movement:
- Slow push-ins and pans for archival/illustrative scenes.
- Staggered reveals for labels and process stages.
- Draw-on strokes for whiteboard diagrams and maps.
- Morphs and shape transformations for concepts and character evolution.
- Exploded separation and reassembly for 3D breakdowns.
- Pixel-by-pixel reveals or limited sprite movement for pixel-art scenes.
- Bar growth, number count-ups, and marker movement for data graphics.
- Clean cuts or short crossfades between distinct visual styles.
- Keep transitions short and subordinate to the teaching point.

## The 15 visual styles in the reference

Treat these as a style library. The Chinese headings below are transcribed from the visible chapter cards; English descriptions are functional translations, not official translations.

1. **皮影讲典故 — Shadow-puppet storytelling**
   - Miniature proscenium/stage, cut-out silhouettes, layered paper characters, warm stage lighting.
   - Best for legends, historical anecdotes, fables, character-driven stories.
2. **等距流程 — Isometric process**
   - Clean isometric 3D/2.5D scene with roads, buildings, vehicles, containers, or objects arranged as a process.
   - Best for logistics, workflows, systems, journeys, step-by-step explanations.
3. **比例尺对比 — Scale comparison**
   - Dark navy/black space with isolated objects, clear scale cue, and small reference marker.
   - Best for planetary size, microscopic/macroscopic comparisons, distance, magnitude, proportions.
4. **白板手绘 — Whiteboard drawing**
   - Off-white sheet or clean whiteboard, thin black hand-drawn lines, simple figures, bars, arrows, labels.
   - Best for explaining logic, derivations, cause/effect, abstract reasoning.
5. **汉字演变 — Character evolution**
   - Warm, textured archival surface, close-up calligraphy/inscription, sequential glyph transformations.
   - Best for language history, symbols, etymology, writing systems, cultural history.
6. **3D拆解 — 3D breakdown**
   - Neutral studio environment, realistic object rendered as a clean 3D model with component callouts.
   - Best for product anatomy, devices, mechanisms, components, internal structure.
7. **像素典故 — Pixel-art anecdote**
   - Low-resolution pixel art, limited palette, blocky characters, side-scrolling environment.
   - Best for gaming culture, retro computing, playful historical anecdotes.
8. **绘本科普 — Picture-book science**
   - Friendly illustrated characters, soft pastel colors, organic shapes, light paper/watercolor feel.
   - Best for biology, nature, child-friendly science, approachable explanations.
9. **数学可视化 — Mathematical visualization**
   - Dark technical canvas, simple geometric shapes, axes, diagrams, formulas, restrained color coding.
   - Best for geometry, statistics, probability, functions, quantitative relationships.
10. **地图动画 — Map animation**
    - Parchment or muted cartographic textures, thin route lines, terrain marks, highlighted locations.
    - Best for historical journeys, migration, battles, exploration, geography, route narratives.
11. **数据信息图 — Data infographic**
    - Deep navy panel, clean bars/columns, large numerals, orange/gold highlights.
    - Best for statistics, rankings, survey results, quantitative summaries.
12. **化学MG — Chemistry motion graphics**
    - Bright, clean educational vector style with simplified molecules, droplets, arrows, labels.
    - Best for chemistry, physical processes, cycles, scientific mechanisms.
13. **卡点金句 — Beat-synced key phrase**
    - Bold editorial typography card, high contrast, oversized key words, colored highlight blocks.
    - Best for memorable conclusion, key definition, quote, turning point.
14. **漫画分格 — Comic panels**
    - Comic grid with black panel borders, speech/caption boxes, sequential reveal.
    - Best for sequential events, dialogue, multiple perspectives, cause/effect.
15. **时间线 — Timeline**
    - Dark navy or charcoal canvas, central axis, large year numerals, milestones.
    - Best for historical development, product evolution, scientific discoveries, chronological narratives.
